# Product

## Register

product

## Users

myK9 serves dog-sport volunteers, exhibitors, judges, stewards, club staff, and site admins. Many users are retired, operating under time pressure, outdoors, at venues with unreliable connectivity, and without deep comfort using software.

Secretaries carry the most operational stress on show day. They need one calm place to see setup, live operations, exceptions, paperwork, and closeout. Exhibitors need quick show discovery, entry, check-in, schedules, notifications, results, and dog history without feeling like they are doing data entry. Judges and stewards need fast, tablet-friendly ringside tools that stay out of the way.

## Product Purpose

myK9 is the complete platform for dog shows before, during, and after the event. It helps clubs run shows, secretaries manage logistics, exhibitors enter and track their dogs, and ringside staff score and coordinate work.

Success means the software disappears behind the task: show staff feel in control, exhibitors feel respected, and the dogs remain the center of attention.

## Brand Personality

Calm. Simple. Respectful.

The product should feel like a competent helper that already understands the dog-show world. It should use dog-show terminology, plain English, and quiet confidence rather than technical jargon or decorative cleverness.

## Anti-references

- Flashy or trendy product UI that competes with the work.
- Dense passive tool palettes that leave a new secretary wondering what to do next.
- Wizard-heavy workflows for interrupt-driven show-day tasks.
- Confirmation dialogs for routine actions.
- Tiny controls, hover-only affordances, gesture-only actions, or text that is hard to read outdoors.
- Technical error messages, sync anxiety, or anything that makes poor connectivity feel like user failure.

## Design Principles

1. The software disappears so the dogs can shine.
2. Each role gets one clear home and one clear next step.
3. Routine show-day changes should feel calm, recoverable, and one or two taps away.
4. Offline is normal, not broken.
5. Plain language beats clever language.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Design for older, non-technical users first. Prefer 44px minimum touch targets, readable 16px+ body text, high-contrast primary content, no hover-only interactions, no gesture-only actions, and calm error states. Motion should communicate state, not decoration. Sync and offline states should be quiet and non-alarming.
